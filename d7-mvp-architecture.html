Part A

The MVP, feature by feature.

Seven features in, one out. Each one names the endpoints it serves, the artifacts it reads, and the tickets standing between it and done.

1

Entity resolution — the ultimate-parent spine.

Live · v1  ·  v2 barely started

Every number in the product is keyed on a consolidated corporate parent. Entity resolution collapses "SAMSUNG ELECTRONICS CO., LTD." and its fifteen spellings and subsidiaries into one parent_key. Everything downstream — the cube, the company file, strength cohorts, EDGAR signals — inherits that answer, which is why it is called the spine and why an error here is an error everywhere.

Serving now
/v1/entities/resolve, /v1/entities/suggest — FTS5 typeahead over ~25M parents, plus the parent_key dimension baked into every cube rollup.
Artifacts
entity_dim.sqlite (derived from the cube), cube_parent_map / cube_entity inside cube.duckdb, assignee_map.
Capability
entity-resolve — live.
What v1 is
Name normalisation + an override map, projected into the cube at build time. It works, and it is what the whole product runs on today.
What v2 adds
A real ownership graph — GLEIF LEI parent relationships, Wikidata, Japan houjin-bangou, Korea DART — with multi-source merge and conflict resolution, so assignee_map and the cube become projections of a graph rather than the source of truth.

Patent lookup — record, graph, text, prosecution.

Live

Point lookups over ~148M resolved patents: one publication by number, its family, its citations both directions, its full text, its prosecution history, its chain of title, and a composed "case experience" view. All SQLite B-tree seeks — millisecond latency, because DuckDB builds the lookups, it doesn't serve them.

Serving now
13 routes across the patent record, the citation graph, full text, prosecution, examiner/art-unit stats and assignments.
Artifacts
lookup_lean.db (required — it is the existence oracle), patent_detail.sqlite, patent_text.sqlite, prosecution.sqlite, oa_timeline.sqlite, examiner.sqlite, assignment.v4.sqlite, app_bridge.sqlite.

4

Keyword & entity search — including the company picker.

Live description search absent

BM25 relevance search over the patent corpus via a Rust Tantivy sidecar, with a two-mode query dialect (simple and expert operators). Alongside it, the typo-tolerant company picker that turns what a user types into a parent_key — the entry point to nearly every other feature.

Serving now
/v1/search, /v1/search/npl + /npl/{work_id} (scholarly prior art), /v1/entities/resolve, /v1/entities/suggest (score + matched_on basis).
Artifacts
Tantivy index (308.9 GiB), the NPL Tantivy index + npl_lookup.sqlite, entity_dim.sqlite.
Capabilities
keyword-search · npl-search · entity-resolve live. description-search is the one absent-by-design capability — it flips to live when description fields are indexed, and /v1/capabilities declares that rather than making clients probe for 503s.
The disk reality
308.9 GiB of index against ~309 GiB free on the serve box — a margin of about 0.05%. A restore only fits because it deletes the stale index before streaming the new one. A 2× transition does not fit.
Tickets to done

The dependency that bit us

Three serving artifacts are re-keys of the cube. entity_dim.sqlite, company_file.sqlite and facet_leaderboard.sqlite are all derived from cube.duckdb. Rebuild the cube and deploy it without rebuilding them, and each one keeps serving counts for the previous corpus — with no error, no 503, and no visible symptom except two numbers on the same screen disagreeing.

That is exactly how it surfaced: a 17-row NOVARTIS delta between /v1/entities/resolve's n_patents and the freshly-built by=tech facet. A stale sidecar does not look broken. It looks like a bug in whichever number you trust less.

The fix is a reconciliation gate per re-key, run at deploy time against a marquee value that must be equal on both sides. Below is what each gate compares and what happens when it fails.

Fig. 3 — The three coupling gates. One self-heals; two refuse the deploy.

The asymmetry is deliberate and worth internalising. entity_dim is a name index — a streaming FTS5 build over the parent set, bounded RAM, minutes — so the serve box can regenerate it in place from the cube already sitting there. company_file and facet_leaderboard are corpus-scale scans and sorts; healing those on the serve box would evict the page cache out from under live traffic. So they fail the deploy instead, and an operator rebuilds on a rented box. Never build on the serve box is not a style preference; it is why those two gates refuse rather than fix.

The egress trap. "R2 egress is free" is true when pulling: a build box can stream terabytes out of R2 for nothing. Pushing the finished artifact out of the build box's own cloud is billed by that provider, and on a large artifact it dominates the run — measured on the 309 GB search index: ~$21 egress against ~$4 for the box itself. bdds ops fire prints the projected publish egress before you spend it.

Contract drift — worth knowing before you trust the spec

The published openapi.json declares 72 /v1 paths. The router registers 73 paths / 74 method+path pairs. The gap is exactly the two key mutations above: POST /v1/admin/keys (an undeclared method on a declared path) and DELETE /v1/admin/keys/{id} (an undeclared path outright). Both are admin-only and gated behind Caddy, so nothing public is undeclared — but the contract is not a complete description of the router.

Separately, #770 is open: the contract under-declares response fields by 73 (56 company-file · 12 hybrid-search · 4 emerging · 1 prior-art), and bdds contract verify exits 1 on it today. The fix direction is settled — derive the contract from the Go types by reflection rather than maintaining a parallel copy (#352, the system-catalog epic, is the durable version of this).

Where the surface is public. Cloudflare → Caddy gates api.tap-ip.com down to /v1, /health and /openapi.json. The /docs Swagger UI, /ui, /admin and /jobs pages are auth-exempt and inject the API key into the page — which is precisely why they stay box-private and are reached over an SSH tunnel, never exposed.

Three things to take away

If you remember nothing else.

1 · One artifact is the keying authority. The cube decides what a company is, and three other serving artifacts are re-keys of it. Any change to how the cube keys parents desynchronises all three, silently. That is why there is a reconciliation gate per re-key, and why one of them self-heals while two refuse the deploy.

2 · Absent is not zero. Every degraded route returns a typed 503 and declares itself on /v1/capabilities. An empty 200 would be a claim about the world; a 503 is a claim about the deployment. The litigation endpoint is the clearest example — cut from the MVP, and honest about it.

3 · Status lives in the running system, not the tracker. Epics stay open after their surface ships. When you need to know whether something works, read /v1/capabilities, the route table, and the capability registry — then go look at the tickets to find out what is still missing.
