The AI layer

AI runs in both places.

TAP•IP uses AI heavily — for drafting patent claims, summarizing prior art, parsing invoices, helping brainstorm sessions, and ten other surfaces. The AI work splits between TypeScript and Python by a clean rule:

TS · stateless
One-shot prompts.

When the AI just needs to answer one question once. Quick. No tools, no memory.

Lives in: frontend/src/lib/ai/
Helpers: complete_json(), complete_text(), complete_vision()

Python · stateful
Agentic loops.

When the AI needs tools, memory, multi-step reasoning. Long-running. Has a tool registry.

Lives in: backend/paralegal/
Helper: agent_runner.run_agent()

The rule: if it's a single prompt → response, use TypeScript. If the AI needs to call tools, remember context across turns, or make decisions in a loop, use Python.

There's also a third piece: the Document RAG microservice at backend/document_rag/. It's a separate FastAPI service for "ask a question about a patent PDF" — it indexes documents into a vector store and answers questions with citations. Lives on its own Railway service so PDF processing doesn't slow down the main app.

When you read code references to complete_json, tool_to_schema, run_agent, or agent_context — those are the canonical AI primitives. CLAUDE.md §10 has the full architectural rules; you don't need them yet, but recognize the names.

For your own project

This is exactly what you'd build.

For most web apps you'd ever build, the stack is just browser + Next.js + Postgres. No Python sidecar unless you're doing heavy AI work. Supabase gives you the database for free up to 500MB. Vercel or Railway runs Next.js for free at small scale.

The total cost of running a real product on this stack at small scale: $0/month. That's not marketing — that's what most indie hackers actually pay.
